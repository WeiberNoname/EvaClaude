# Chapter 01 — The Boy Beneath the Giant

Status: story proposal for review. No implementation changes made for this draft.

## Scope

Use the original television continuity. Shinji's first Angel opponent is Sachiel, designated the Third Angel. The proposed chapter combines the essential arrival and recruitment events of episode 1 with the first battle's resolution from episode 2. This gives the short chapter an ending. A strictly episode-1-only version would end at deployment.

Cut or merge approximately 60–70% of supporting material. This is an editorial scope target, not a measured runtime reduction. Keep the emotional progression: isolation, fear, rejection, reluctant responsibility, loss of control, and uncertain survival.

The story below is condensed adaptation prose with original connective writing, not a transcript or shot-for-shot recreation.

## Short story

Shinji arrives in an evacuated Tokyo-3 carrying a small bag and a summons from the father who has barely been part of his life. The public telephone gives him nothing. Somewhere down the empty street, an evacuation announcement begins again.

Then the pavement trembles.

Between the buildings, a pale face rises above the rooftops. Shinji cannot tell whether it is looking at him. Military fire crosses the sky. The creature keeps walking. A shock wave breaks the windows around him, and he drops behind the telephone booth with his hands over his head.

A car brakes beside the curb. Misato calls him over. He runs through falling glass and gets inside. As they descend toward shelter, he watches the creature disappear behind the buildings. He has come here to see his father. Nobody has explained why his father needed him today.

The answer waits underground.

Misato brings him to a vast chamber containing the face of another giant. Purple armor disappears into darkness and restraints. High above the platform, Gendo tells Shinji that he must pilot it.

Shinji refuses. He has never seen this machine before. His father offers an order where he expected a welcome.

An injured girl is brought forward to replace him. Rei tries to rise despite the pain. Another impact shakes the chamber; debris falls, and the restrained Eva moves to shield Shinji. He looks from its enormous hand to Rei struggling on the stretcher. He is still terrified when he agrees to enter.

Inside the entry plug, liquid rises around him. His first breath feels impossible. Then he can breathe, and the darkness becomes a view of the city. Misato's voice guides him through the launch.

He returns to the street at the height of its rooftops. Sachiel is waiting.

Shinji tries to make Unit-01 take a step. The body lurches. He attempts to defend himself, but the Angel closes the distance and overwhelms him. Pain runs through a limb that should belong to the machine. The radio dissolves into alarms, then silence.

Unit-01 moves again without waiting for him.

From inside, Shinji catches broken impressions: pavement rushing beneath him, an unfamiliar roar, resistance tearing apart. The Eva forces its way through Sachiel's defensive field. Trapped in the struggle, the Angel folds around it and detonates.

Shinji wakes in a hospital bed. Misato tells him the Angel is gone. He asks what happened after the radio stopped. She does not answer immediately.

Beyond the window, recovery crews work beneath a damaged skyline. He listens to a machine beside the bed and tries to remember whether the breathing he heard in the cockpit was his own.

## What remains essential

- See Sachiel first from vulnerable human scale, then confront it from inside Unit-01.
- Establish Misato as the person trying to reach Shinji and Gendo as the person demanding something from him.
- Preserve Rei's injury and the Eva's unexplained protective movement as the turning point.
- Let Shinji remain inexperienced and frightened during the battle.
- End with survival and uncertainty about the Eva, leaving room for another chapter.

## What is removed or merged

- Military strategy meetings, repeated attack attempts, and extended weapon demonstrations become one brief failed military response.
- Most of the car journey, facility travel, elevator conversations, and technical briefings become short transitions.
- Secondary staff introductions and background lore are deferred.
- Domestic comedy, apartment settling-in, school material, and future plot threads are outside this chapter.
- The battle and hospital aftermath follow chronological order instead of the television episode's flashback arrangement.

## Consequences for the existing prototype

The current floating geometric enemy is a placeholder and does not represent Sachiel. The current practiced combat style, particle lance, and visible synchronization score should not determine this chapter's story. Their replacement or reuse belongs in the game plan after story review.
